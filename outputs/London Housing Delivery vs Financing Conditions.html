Where London Housing Delivery Stalls

Planning permission is not the same as construction. We examine 26,961 London residential planning permissions decided between 2018 and 2023 and ask whether schemes facing different financing conditions have different rates of starting construction within three years.

THE QUESTION

Planning permission is not the same as housing delivery.

A scheme can receive planning permission without immediately becoming a construction project. We therefore follow schemes after permission and ask a simple question: did construction actually start within three years?

WHY THREE YEARS?

A common observation window

Every permission receives the same three-year window in which to observe whether construction begins. This makes different approval cohorts more comparable and avoids treating a very recent permission as a failure simply because it has not had enough time to progress.

WHAT IS RATE EXPOSURE?

The financing environment after permission

Schemes are grouped according to the change in mortgage rates they experienced during the following three years. "Rising most" means the largest increases in this dataset; "Falling most" means the largest decreases.

WHAT WE FIND

Four patterns stand out

54.2%

Across 26,961 schemes, 54.2% recorded a construction start within three years of planning permission.

The observed start rate falls from 60.0% in the "Falling most" group to 46.7% in the "Rising most" group.

Borough-level start rates range widely, showing that financing conditions cannot explain all of the variation in delivery.

HOW SHOULD WE INTERPRET THIS?

The relationship is interesting — but it is not a causal estimate.

Schemes facing larger increases in mortgage rates have lower observed construction-start rates. This is consistent with financing conditions being associated with development delivery.

But interest rates do not move in isolation. Construction costs, housing demand, land values, economic conditions and local scheme characteristics can change at the same time.

What we can say:
Schemes exposed to different financing environments have different observed construction-start rates.
What we cannot say from this analysis:
That a particular increase in interest rates caused a particular scheme not to start.
AN IMPORTANT CAVEAT

Recent permissions have not had as much time to progress.

A scheme approved in 2023 cannot be interpreted in exactly the same way as one approved in 2018. The later scheme has had less time to move through planning, financing and construction.

This is why the analysis uses a fixed three-year observation window rather than simply asking whether a scheme has ever started or completed.

METHOD

How the analysis was constructed

Population

26,961 London residential planning permissions decided between 2018 and 2023.

Outcome

Whether a scheme recorded a construction start within three years of its planning decision.

Financing exposure

Schemes are grouped into five exposure groups according to mortgage-rate change during the following three years.

Geography

Start rates are compared across London boroughs. Boroughs with fewer than 100 decisions are excluded from the borough comparison.

NEXT STEP

What would we do next?

  • Control for scheme size, tenure and borough characteristics.
  • Separate the association with financing conditions from construction-cost changes.
  • Use survival analysis to account more explicitly for schemes that have not yet had enough time to complete.
  • Investigate whether planning-process events, such as amendments or condition discharges, provide early signals of delivery risk.

Source: uploaded schemes_with_market.csv for this session. "Started within 3y" and the rate, cost and sales-change fields are used as provided in the dataset. The analysis is descriptive and does not by itself establish causality. Rate and cost changes are correlated, so separating their individual associations would require additional controls and modelling.
